My Seventh Meal

A confirmation-first nutrition product concept for South Asian home-cooked food. The architecture rejects the false precision of photo-only calorie estimates: multimodal AI identifies plausible components and uncertainty, users answer the smallest set of high-impact questions, and a deterministic nutrition service calculates a traceable range.

The product problem

Mainstream calorie trackers work best when foods have standardized labels, weights, or restaurant entries. South Asian home cooking frequently varies by household, region, oil or ghee, cream, sugar, serving vessel, and mixed-plate composition. A photograph may identify dal and rice; it cannot reliably reveal the recipe, yield, hidden fat, or precise portion. Manual recipe entry is more accurate but too slow for frequent logging.

Recognize without pretending certainty

The vision layer returns one to three likely dishes or components, visible portion cues, cooking-method clues, and explicit uncertainty tags—not final nutrients.

Ask only what changes the answer

A question engine prioritizes portion, oil or ghee, key ingredient, cooking style, and household or restaurant variant, targeting a normal median of two questions.

Calculate from structured inputs

Confirmed foods, ingredients, recipes, yield, and serving amount feed versioned deterministic logic with data provenance and lower/upper ranges.

Remember the household

Corrections become reusable meal and recipe profiles so “Mom’s poha” or a family rajma configuration becomes faster and more consistent over time.

Proposed technical stack

React Native + Expo

One iOS and Android codebase for camera capture, guided confirmation, recent meals, dashboards, and staged beta distribution.

Python FastAPI

Typed services for AI orchestration, structured-output validation, nutrition calculation, data quality, and account workflows.

Supabase + PostgreSQL

Managed authentication, relational recipe and meal data, row-level security, signed media access, export, and deletion requests.

Provider-abstracted AI

A cost-aware routing layer can use a lower-cost multimodal model for ordinary meals, escalate ambiguity, validate schemas, cache recurring meals, and avoid permanent vendor lock-in.

USDA + curated South Asian data

FoodData Central supplies foundational nutrient records; curated ingredients, dish templates, household recipes, versions, and provenance cover the cultural long tail.

GitHub Actions + Expo EAS

Automated tests, staged environments, mobile builds, release gates, and synthetic-data-only staging support controlled delivery.

Data and evaluation strategy

  • Layered food model: foundational nutrient data, curated South Asian ingredients, versioned recipe templates, household recipes, user corrections, and source/confidence metadata.
  • Controlled evaluation set: at least 500 photos covering 100 high-frequency dishes, different lighting and plate styles, mixed meals, leftovers, restaurant food, and home food.
  • Separate quality measures: top-three dish presence, question quality, high-impact ingredient recall, calculation reproducibility, and the difference between photo-only and confirmed estimates.
  • Calculation regression tests: known recipes and yield totals protect deterministic nutrient logic when data or formulas change.
  • Data-quality operations: every curated recipe has an owner, source, version, date, yield, review status, and error queue.

Validation gates

Problem and price

Interviews, diary studies, a clickable confirmation prototype, real price options, and paid commitments must demonstrate repeated pain before a full build.

Functional proof

The ordinary flow targets 45 seconds or less, top-three candidate recall of at least 75%, a median of two or fewer questions, and repeated test-group usage.

Unit economics

Model routing, caching, and escalation must show a credible path below the planned AI/photo cost ceiling per active paid user.

Commercial release

Production authentication, subscriptions, export and deletion, data review, app-store declarations, privacy materials, and security checks come only after functional evidence.

Safety and privacy decisions

General wellness, not diagnosis

The language uses estimates, sources, limitations, and neutral habit guidance. Disease management, treatment claims, medication advice, and medical-grade accuracy are outside version one.

Minimize sensitive data

Use signed uploads, strip image metadata, separate identity from health records, redact logs, and keep meal, weight, and photo content out of product-analytics payloads.

Retention by design

Raw meal images have a defined deletion target unless the user explicitly chooses to retain them; primary data, derived data, backups, and processors need deletion SLAs.

Non-exploitative behavior design

No shame, fear, extreme restriction, or unsafe rapid-loss messaging. Users should be able to hide weight-centric elements and pause goals or reminders.
