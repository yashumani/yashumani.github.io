The product problem

A meaningful place—a first home, proposal spot, hometown, trip, or family landmark—can be emotionally powerful but difficult to turn into a polished physical or digital keepsake. The product had to make location discovery easy, keep map customization approachable on a phone, preserve exact design state, and create a credible path to purchase without introducing an expensive custom backend too early.

Find almost any meaningful place

Visitors can use a built-in international city catalog, Wikipedia-coordinate search, browser geolocation, or manual latitude and longitude.

Design without specialist tools

Editable copy, layouts, formats, map moods, marker and label controls, pan, zoom, and recenter are organized into a guided creator.

Preserve the exact composition

Drafts autosave locally, shared links encode compact design state in the URL hash, and cart items retain restorable design snapshots.

Convert without unsafe payment handling

The site delegates card collection to a hosted provider and offers a no-payment order-request fallback while seller links remain blank.

Interaction and performance decisions

Defer the expensive map runtime

The first screen does not load MapLibre or its stylesheet. They arrive as the creator becomes relevant or the visitor begins a creator action.

Ship stable first paint

A deterministic Node build combines maintainable source styles into the single production stylesheet served by public pages, reducing runtime layout movement.

Gate motion by visibility

Scroll effects use visibility checks, batch updates through requestAnimationFrame, and switch off for reduced-motion preferences.

Keep core commerce in static HTML

Product comparison, cart recovery, checkout details, FAQ, and policy links are present before JavaScript runs, improving resilience and discoverability.

Implemented product capabilities

  • Place discovery: six one-click landing presets, 50+ built-in cities, global Wikipedia-coordinate lookup, geolocation, and manual coordinates.
  • Poster creator: editable title, place line, date, heading, dedication, seven story presets, five map moods, three layouts, and three formats.
  • Map controls: direct pan and zoom, marker and label toggles, and recentering.
  • Persistence and sharing: automatic draft saving and compact stateful links through the URL hash.
  • Preview output: watermarked PNG and browser print/PDF previews.
  • Commerce: three product tiers, exact-snapshot cart items, multiple designs per cart, editing, removal, subtotal, recovery, and backup/restore.
  • Launch trust: privacy, terms, refunds, and delivery pages; no card data, custom database, or analytics collection.

Commerce safety model

Hosted payment only

No private payment secret belongs in the static repository. Public Payhip or equivalent product URLs are configuration, not hardcoded credentials.

Restorable order brief

Every cart item carries a design link and human-readable product, price, location, coordinate, and personalization details for fulfillment.

No false payment confirmation

The return page accepts current and legacy local order shapes for continuity, but local data never proves that a provider charged the customer.

Deliberate MVP boundary

Automatic payment verification and file delivery require a webhook and small backend in a later commercial phase.
