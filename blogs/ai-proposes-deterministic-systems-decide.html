Working paper / Applied AI architecture

AI Proposes, Deterministic Systems Decide

A reusable architecture pattern for combining probabilistic AI with user confirmation, policy, versioned truth services, provenance, and safe operational boundaries.

PublishedAugust 12, 2026Last reviewedAugust 12, 2026StatusWorking paper

Thesis. Probabilistic models should reduce ambiguity and propose structure, while confirmed inputs and versioned deterministic services own calculations, authorization, persistence, and release.

01Abstract

Generative and multimodal models are good at proposing candidates, interpreting unstructured inputs, and prioritizing questions. They are weaker as the final authority for calculations, entitlements, access decisions, payments, releases, or other outputs that must be reproducible and governed.

This paper presents a reusable architecture pattern: AI proposes, a user or policy resolves uncertainty, and a deterministic service decides. The pattern preserves the value of AI while keeping the final truth path inspectable.

02The pattern

ObserveCollect an image, document, event, request, or analytical context.
ProposeUse AI to generate candidates, structure, uncertainty, or high-value questions.
ConfirmResolve ambiguous facts through user input, policy, trusted data, or review.
DecideUse versioned deterministic logic to calculate, authorize, persist, or release.

The proposal layer is probabilistic. The decision layer should be explicit about inputs, rules, versions, and provenance.

03Why the model should not be the final calculator

A language or vision model can produce a plausible number without preserving the ingredient records, units, formula, rounding rules, or source version that generated it. The number may look precise while remaining difficult to reproduce.

A deterministic truth service can instead store the structured inputs and apply versioned rules. The model helps reduce entry friction; it does not erase the calculation boundary.

04Candidate generation

The proposal service should return structured candidates rather than persuasive prose. Useful fields include:

  • Candidate label or classification.
  • Confidence or uncertainty indicators.
  • Visible evidence.
  • Missing facts that materially affect the decision.
  • Recommended clarification questions.
  • Provider and model metadata for traceability.

Schema validation belongs immediately after inference.

05Clarification should maximize information gain

Do not ask every possible question. Ask the smallest set likely to change the result. In nutrition, oil, yield, portion, and recipe variant may matter more than garnish. In finance, metric version, entity, period, and target basis may matter more than display format.

The clarification policy can be deterministic, model-assisted, or hybrid, but its objective should be explicit: reduce decision-relevant uncertainty at acceptable user cost.

06The deterministic truth service

The final service should own:

  • Canonical entities and identifiers.
  • Validated units and types.
  • Versioned formulas and policy rules.
  • Rounding and range logic.
  • Provenance and effective dates.
  • Idempotency where actions may be retried.
  • Audit records for material decisions.

The service may still consume model-derived fields, but only after those fields are validated and accepted as inputs.

07Ranges are often more honest than single values

When uncertainty remains, the deterministic layer can calculate a bounded range from confirmed inputs rather than allowing the model to invent false precision. The range should explain which assumptions drive the lower and upper bounds.

This approach is useful for nutrition estimates, operational forecasts, cost ranges, risk scenarios, and incomplete-data analysis.

08Learning without erasing confirmation

User corrections can improve future proposals: a household recipe, repeated merchant mapping, preferred hierarchy, recurring metric, or common serving size. The learning loop should preserve consent, scope, versioning, and an ability to correct or delete memory.

Personalization should reduce repeated questions, not silently promote previous guesses into permanent truth.

09Safety, privacy, and current guidance

For a general-wellness nutrition product, the January 2026 FDA guidance clarifies the low-risk general-wellness boundary and the distinction from diagnosis, treatment, mitigation, or prevention claims. USDA FoodData Central provides API-accessible nutrient data with documented endpoints, key handling, rate limits, and public-domain licensing. The FTC’s amended Health Breach Notification Rule underscores that many health apps and similar technologies outside HIPAA can still carry breach-notification obligations.

NIST’s AI RMF and Generative AI Profile support lifecycle risk management, testing, evaluation, provenance, and incident practices around the probabilistic layer.

10Applications beyond nutrition

  • Finance: AI identifies candidate drivers; governed metric logic calculates variance and aggregation.
  • Document processing: AI extracts fields; schema and policy services validate and authorize downstream use.
  • Research: AI drafts claims; evidence graphs and critique gates decide whether publication is allowed.
  • Commerce: AI helps configure a product; deterministic checkout and payment verification decide order state.
  • Privacy: AI classifies content; access-control policy and human review decide release.

11Failure modes

The pattern fails when confirmation is ceremonial, deterministic logic is undocumented, user corrections are treated as ground truth without scope, or the model’s confidence is mistaken for calibrated probability. It also fails when the “deterministic” layer merely hides hardcoded assumptions.

Trust comes from visible boundaries, versioned rules, tests, and honest uncertainty—not from replacing one opaque component with another.

12Conclusion

AI is most useful when it handles ambiguity without becoming the unreviewed authority for consequential truth. Candidate generation and clarification can remain probabilistic. Calculation, authorization, persistence, and release should be governed by explicit systems.

The pattern is simple enough to remember and broad enough to reuse: AI proposes. People and policy confirm. Deterministic services decide. The system records why.
