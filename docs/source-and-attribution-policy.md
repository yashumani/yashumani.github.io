# Source and attribution policy

Last reviewed: August 13, 2026

This portfolio distinguishes four kinds of material.

## 1. Original project work

A project page may claim authorship for product decisions, implementation, integration, testing, diagrams, and written synthesis that can be traced to the associated repository or documented work product.

A project page must not imply that the author invented a standard, research method, open-source library, vendor capability, or established industry pattern.

## 2. External prior art and published guidance

When a claim depends on a company publication, standard, research paper, official product documentation, or established method, the page should name and link the source.

Prefer primary sources:

- the standard-setting body;
- the original research publication;
- official vendor or project documentation;
- the original engineering publication;
- the canonical open-source repository.

Secondary summaries may be used only when a primary source is unavailable or when the secondary analysis itself is the subject.

## 3. Implementation evidence

Repositories, live demos, tests, architecture files, runbooks, and dated review documents establish what this portfolio implementation currently contains. They do not by themselves prove business impact, production readiness, adoption, or causality.

Metrics and maturity labels must be dated or traceable to a current source snapshot.

## 4. Personal judgment and synthesis

Personal conclusions, proposed frameworks, and operating preferences do not require an external citation when they are clearly presented as the author’s view. They must not be written as settled research or universal fact.

## Citation placement

Each working paper keeps a references section. Each project case study keeps a Sources and attribution section near the end, plus implementation links near the top.

A source note should explain why the source is relevant. A list of unexplained links is not enough.

## Fact-check workflow

Before a dated release:

1. Separate factual claims from interpretation.
2. Trace project-state claims to the current repository, demo, test result, or review document.
3. Search for established prior art behind methods, frameworks, and vendor capabilities.
4. Replace secondary sources with primary sources where possible.
5. Check that links resolve and that time-sensitive guidance is still current.
6. Remove or qualify unsupported impact, security, production, medical, legal, and causal claims.
7. Record the review date.

External citation does not imply endorsement, partnership, or ownership by the cited organization.
